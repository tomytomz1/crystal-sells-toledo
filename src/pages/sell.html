The honest version

Most homes leave money on the table before they ever hit the market.

Not because of the market. Because of three avoidable mistakes.

Listing before it is ready

The first ten days generate the most showings a home will ever get. Going live with unfinished paint and a cluttered garage spends that attention on a version of the house nobody wants to buy.

Pricing on hope

An optimistic price does not attract a higher offer — it attracts silence, then a price cut, and price cuts read as “something is wrong with it.”

Marketing that stops at the MLS

Entering a listing into the system is the bare minimum. It is not a marketing plan.

Start to finish

How your sale actually goes

Six stages, grouped into the three phases every sale passes through.

Before market

Stages 01–03

01

The walkthrough — free, no commitment

We walk the house together, room by room. I tell you what buyers in your price band will notice, what is worth fixing, and—just as important—what to leave alone. You get a written list. You are under no obligation to list with me, or at all.

02

A price built from the last 90 days

Not a computer estimate. Real closed comparables from your streets, adjusted for condition, lot, updates and the things an algorithm cannot see. You will see the same data I see and understand exactly why the number is the number.

03

Prep, stage and shoot

Repairs coordinated with trades I trust, staging advice using what you already own, then a professional shoot: daylight stills, twilight exteriors, walkthrough video and a measured floor plan. Everything ready before day one.

On market

Stages 04–05

04

Launch, loudly

MLS and full syndication, paid social targeted at buyers actively searching your area, an email push to my agent network, a broker preview and open houses that are actually promoted rather than just scheduled.

05

Offers, negotiated properly

Every offer broken down beyond the headline price — financing strength, appraisal risk, inspection exposure, timelines. The highest number is not always the best offer, and you will know why before you sign.
