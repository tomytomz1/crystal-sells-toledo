The honest version

Three things to work through before you list

Preparation and pricing choices deserve attention before your home goes on the market.

Listing before it is ready

If repairs or cleaning are unfinished, early visitors may see a home that does not present as well as it could. We decide what needs attention before setting a launch date.

Pricing on hope

A price above what comparable sales support may limit interest. We review the evidence before listing, and revisit the price if buyer response calls for it.

Marketing that stops at the MLS

Entering a listing into the system is the bare minimum. It is not a marketing plan.

Start to finish

How your sale actually goes

Six stages, grouped into the three phases every sale passes through.

Before market

Stages 01–03

01

The walkthrough — free, no commitment

We walk the house together, room by room. I tell you what buyers in your price range will notice, what is worth fixing, and—just as important—what to leave alone. You get a written list. You are under no obligation to list with me, or at all.

02

A price range based on relevant sales

I review recent sales of comparable homes, widening the search when needed. We look at condition, lot, updates and current competition, and I explain how the evidence supports the range.

03

Prep, stage and shoot

Repairs coordinated with contractors I trust, staging advice using what you already own, then the photography and video agreed in your plan. Everything ready before day one.

On market

Stages 04–05

04

Launch, loudly

Your listing is shared through the MLS and participating listing sites, with additional promotion as agreed in your marketing plan. That can include paid social aimed at buyers searching your area, an email to my agent network, a preview for other agents, and promoted open houses.

05

Offers, negotiated properly

Every offer broken down past the headline price — how solid the buyer’s financing is, whether the appraisal is likely to come in short, what the inspection could open up, and the dates. The highest number is not always the best offer, and you will know why before you sign.

Inspection, appraisal, lender, title. I chase every deadline so you do not have to, and you hear from me before you have to wonder.
