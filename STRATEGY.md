hours.** Every tactic here is chosen for return per hour, not for looking busy.

## 2. The one number to manage

**Listings taken per month.** Not closings, not GCI, not leads.

Listings are the only lagging metric a listing agent can actually control, and they compound:
every sign in a yard generates calls from neighbors, every listing produces a review, and
every review makes the next listing appointment easier to win.

```
Year 1 target     8–12 listings taken     ≈ 1 per month
Year 2 target    18–24 listings taken     ≈ 2 per month   ← this is #1 territory
```

At ~$400K average and a 2.5% listing side, twenty listings a year is roughly **$200,000 in
gross commission before splits** — the number that ends the gig work.

Track it on one line in a spreadsheet. Everything else in this document is a means to
that number.

## 3. Phase 0 — Foundation (weeks 1–4, ~15 hours total)

Do these once. They pay out for years.

- [ ] **Google Business Profile, claimed and completed.** This is the single highest-return
      free asset in local real estate. Category "Real Estate Agent", service area Perrysburg
      + 43551, real photos, correct hours. More people will find Crystal here than through

- [ ] **Launch the website** with real photos and a working form (see `README.md`).
- [ ] **Submit to Google Search Console** and confirm the sitemap is indexed.
- [ ] **A CRM with every contact in it.** Any CRM. The brokerage's is fine. What matters is
      that all 150–300 people Crystal knows are in one place with a tag.
- [ ] **A review pipeline.** One saved text message, sent the day after every closing, with
      a direct link to the Google review form. Reviews are the whole ballgame for a new agent
      and they have to be asked for at the exact moment of maximum goodwill.
- [ ] **Zillow and Realtor.com profiles**, complete, linked to the site.
- [ ] **Set the calendar.** Two protected prospecting blocks a week, 90 minutes each, in the
      calendar as immovable appointments. Not "when there's time" — there is never time.

## 4. Phase 1 — The sphere (months 1–4)

**The first eight listings come from people who already know Crystal.** They will not come
from strangers, ads, or Zillow leads. Every new agent who fails does so by skipping this.

- **Announce it properly, once.** A personal message — not a mass post — to all 150 contacts.
  Not "I'm in real estate now, let me know if you need anything" (which asks nothing and gets
  nothing) but "I've joined Key Realty and I'm focusing on Perrysburg. Who's the first person
  you'd think of who might move in the next year?"
- **Then stay in front of them monthly.** One genuinely useful email a month: what sold on
  their street, what it means, one thing they could do about it. Twelve touches a year beats
  one heroic announcement.
- **Say yes to the small stuff.** The $180K starter home, the rental referral, the friend's
  cousin who "just has a question." These are not distractions in year one — they are
  reputation, reps and reviews.
- **Ask for a review after every single transaction.** Target 20 Google reviews by month 12.
  Most agents in this market have fewer than that after a decade.

**Time cost: about 4 hours a week.** Mostly during the two prospecting blocks.

## 5. Phase 2 — Own a micro-farm (months 3–18)

This is the core of the plan, and the part that is genuinely mom-compatible.

Crystal should not try to cover all of 43551. She should pick **one subdivision of 300–500
homes** and become the only agent anyone there can name.

### Picking it

In the MLS, for each 43551 subdivision, calculate:

```
turnover rate = homes sold in last 12 months ÷ total homes
```

Choose an area where:
- turnover is **4% or better** (a 400-home area selling 16+ homes a year),
- **no single agent holds more than ~20%** of those sales,
- Crystal has some genuine connection — she lives there, her kids go to school with those
  kids, she has already sold one.

**Do not pick the area the #1 agent already dominates.** Win an empty one first.

At 4% turnover, a 400-home farm produces ~16 listings a year. Capturing a third of that is
5–6 listings — half of a year-one target from one area.

### Working it (the whole routine, ~3 hours a month)

| Cadence | Action | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | One postcard or letter to all 400 homes: what sold, for how much, in how many days | Homeowners are genuinely nosy about their own street. This is the only "junk mail" people read. |
| Per sale | "Just Listed" and "Just Sold" cards to the 30 nearest homes | Proof, delivered exactly where it is most persuasive |
| Quarterly | A one-page market snapshot for the farm, posted to the site and emailed | Becomes the local reference; feeds the website's SEO |
| Ongoing | Be visibly present — school events, the pool, the neighborhood Facebook group | Recognition is the whole product |

Twelve consistent monthly touches beat a single expensive campaign. **Consistency is the
entire strategy.** An agent who mails 400 homes every month for eighteen months will beat one
who mails 4,000 homes twice.

Budget roughly **$250–400 a month** in print and postage. It is the only spend on this list
that is genuinely required.

## 6. Phase 3 — Be the answer to "what's my home worth?" (ongoing)

The website exists to catch demand that already exists. Two things make it work:

**Hyperlocal content the big brokerages will not bother writing.** The neighborhood guides
already on the site are the skeleton. Fill them with what only a resident knows and add one
new page a month:

- "What homes sold for in Three Meadows this quarter"
- "Village at Riverbend: what to know before buying new construction here"
- "Perrysburg vs. Maumee vs. Sylvania: an honest comparison for people relocating"
- "The five streets in 43551 where homes sell fastest"

Search volume for these is small. It is also almost entirely people who are about to move,
and Crystal is competing against national sites that cannot write them. Two hours a month.

**Reviews, everywhere.** Google, Zillow, Realtor.com, Facebook. A new agent with 25 specific
recent reviews outcompetes a veteran with six vague ones, because that is what a seller
actually checks before calling.

## 7. Phase 4 — Win listing appointments (the skill that decides everything)

Everything above generates appointments. This is where they convert.

The leaders in 43551 average around $400K. Crystal's listing presentation has to be visibly
better than theirs at that price point, and the differentiators are ones a solo agent can
genuinely deliver:

- **Professional photography and video on every listing, at every price.** Most agents in
  this market ration it above $400K. Doing it on a $220K house is a real, visible difference
  and it is what the website promises.
- **A written pre-listing preparation plan.** A room-by-room list, with what each change is
  worth. Nobody else hands the seller a document.
- **Real availability.** A solo agent who answers her own phone beats a team where the seller
  gets handed to an assistant. This is Crystal's genuine structural advantage over the
  brokerages above her — do not let anyone talk her out of it.
- **Weekly written seller updates** with showing feedback and traffic numbers. The most
  common complaint about listing agents, everywhere, is silence.

**Practice the presentation out loud twenty times before the first one.** Including the
pricing conversation, including the commission question. This one skill converts more
appointments than any amount of marketing generates.

## 8. The weekly rhythm

Roughly 20–25 focused hours, structured so that showings and closings fit around school.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Mon AM** — 90 min | Prospecting block: sphere calls and follow-up |
| **Mon PM** — 60 min | Market review: new listings, pendings, closings in the farm |
| **Tue** | Appointments and showings |
| **Wed AM** — 90 min | Prospecting block: farm follow-up, past clients |
| **Wed PM** — 60 min | Content: one website page or one farm mailer |
| **Thu** | Appointments and showings |
| **Fri AM** — 60 min | Admin, CRM hygiene, review requests |
| **Weekend** | Open houses and showings **by appointment only** — not by default |

**Protect the two prospecting blocks above everything else.** They are the only activities on
this list that generate future business rather than service current business, and they are
always the first thing a busy week deletes.

## 11. What to say no to

Being a busy mom is not a handicap here — it forces the discipline most agents never develop.
Protect the hours by declining:

- **Floor duty and open houses for other agents' listings.** Hours spent building someone
  else's business.
- **Marketing spend outside the farm.** Take every deal that comes to you, anywhere in greater
  Toledo — turning away business is not the goal, and referrals are always worth having. What
  should stay disciplined is where the *money and the prospecting hours* go: the mailers, the
  content and the farm work stay pointed at 43551. Serve widely; market narrowly.
- **Paid lead portals, for now.** They convert at low single digits and demand instant
  response times that do not survive a school pickup.
- **Brokerage busywork** that does not produce a listing appointment.
- **Being everywhere on social media.** Pick one platform — realistically Facebook, since
  that is where Perrysburg homeowners actually are — and be consistent there. Ignore the rest.
